# Writing your review prompt

Your weekly review is written by an AI from numbers Health Review computes from your data. The **Prompt** tab holds the instructions it follows: a few short sections about you and how you want the review written. Good sections make a review that sounds like a coach who knows you.

On the Prompt tab, **Help me write a section** asks a few questions and drafts a section for you (with Claude if you use AI, or from a template without it). You review it, change anything, and save. Every save keeps the old version.

## What helps

- **Be specific.** "I lift 4 mornings a week in a home gym" beats "I work out".
- **Say what you want, not just who you are.** "Lead with what went well" or "Nudge me when I skip Zone 2".
- **Keep each section to one topic** and a few sentences. Short and clear works better than long.
- **Update it when life changes:** a new program, an event to train for, a busy month.

## What to leave out

- **Medications, doses, lab values and diagnoses.** They come from the **Meds** and **Labs** tabs, and the review only ever raises them as something to discuss with your physician.
- **Numbers the review should report.** It already has your data; tell it what matters to you instead.

## About me

Who you are and what you want over the next few months.

- I'm in my late 40s, training to stay strong and mobile for the long run. For the next three months I want to squat 300 lb for a single and keep my resting heart rate in the low 50s. I work at a desk, so steps matter on weekdays.
- I'm 34 and training for my first half marathon in May. Strength is second: I lift twice a week to stay injury free. I travel every other week, so some weeks will be lighter.
- I'm 61. My goals are better sleep, losing about 10 lb slowly, and keeping my balance and strength. I walk daily and do a short strength routine three times a week.
- I'm 52, coming back after a few months off. I want consistency more than records: three workouts a week, every week, and back to my old numbers by spring.

## Coaching style

How the review should talk to you.

- Be direct and brief. Start with two or three wins, then what slipped, then exactly three things to do next week. Under 600 words.
- Encouraging but honest. Explain the why behind each suggestion in a sentence. Skip anything that did not change this week.
- Write like a coach texting me: short paragraphs, plain words, no jargon. If one thing matters most this week, say it first.
- Use headings for Training, Cardio and Recovery. Compare this week with my four-week average, and flag anything trending the wrong way for two weeks in a row.

## Training

How you lift.

- I lift 4 days a week (upper, lower, upper, lower) in a home gym: barbell, dumbbells to 75 lb, bands and a power tower. No machines. Avoid suggesting overhead barbell pressing (shoulder).
- I'm following a 12 week strength program. Keep me on the program; suggest swaps only for exercises I skip twice in a row.
- Three full body sessions a week, 45 minutes each. Focus on deadlift and pull-ups. I prefer dumbbells for pressing.

## Cardio and endurance

What cardio you do and what to watch.

- I row three times a week for 30 to 45 minutes and walk daily. Watch my Zone 2 minutes and VO2 max; nudge me if Zone 2 drops below 150 minutes.
- Training for a 10k in June: three runs a week, one long. Watch my long run and resting heart rate, and tell me if I'm doing too much intensity.
- I cycle on weekends and walk at lunch. I care about heart health more than speed.

## Weight and nutrition

Body composition goals and habits (no medical details).

- I want to lose about 10 lb over three months while keeping muscle. Focus on the weekly weight trend, not single days, and my protein and drinks.
- I'm trying to gain muscle slowly: up about 1 lb a month. Tell me if my weight is flat for three weeks.
- I eat well during the week and less well on weekends. Point out patterns between weekend drinks and how I sleep and train.

## Sleep and recovery

- I aim for 7.5 hours. Late workouts and drinks hurt my sleep; point it out when they line up. Watch HRV dips of more than a week.
- I'm a light sleeper with an early alarm. Suggest one small thing to try when a week's sleep is short.
- When my readiness is low two days in a row, suggest an easier day instead of pushing.
